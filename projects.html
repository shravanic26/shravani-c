Research · UCLA · 2026Diet and blood markers in the Aging Adult Brain ConnectomeHabitual diet predicts glucose and inflammation; a single day's recall predicts nothing.

The Aging Adult Brain Connectome is an NIH study following a thousand-plus adults, from their late thirties past 100, at four sites including UCLA. Working with two of its UCLA investigators, Dr. Helen Lavretsky and Dr. Dara Ghahremani, our team asked whether what people report eating in a 24-hour recall shows up in their blood. Across 781 participants I fit mixed-effects models that split each nutrient into a person's habitual intake and that day's deviation from it. Habitual fiber and overall diet quality predicted lower glucose and lower inflammation; a single day's deviation predicted nothing. One recall is noise; averaged across visits, it's a real signal.

R · lme4 · PCA · Quarto
Coursework · STATS 140XP · 2026H-1B visa denial rates, 2017–2023Denials tracked the administration in office more than the job market.

Does an H-1B outcome depend more on politics or on the job market? Six classmates and I merged seven years of USCIS petition data in R and tracked denial rates and new-hire ratios by industry. Denials climbed under the Trump administration and fell after Biden's reversals with no change to the law, and COVID pushed employers toward keeping existing visa holders over sponsoring new ones.

Internship · Kohl's · 2025–26Freight ETA forecastingA two-stage XGBoost model that re-predicts arrival at each stage of transit.

Kohl's was predicting when long-haul freight would arrive with a static 60-day estimate, and it drifted every time a vendor or carrier ran late. Through the Data Science Union, I helped build a two-stage XGBoost model in Python and BigQuery that re-predicts as a shipment moves through each stage of transit, using 20+ engineered features across 5+ datasets. In my own benchmark it beat the carriers' own ETAs by about 70%.

Python · BigQuery · XGBoost
Research · UCLA · 2024–25Bias in the COMPAS algorithmMeasuring, then reducing, racial bias in a recidivism risk score.

COMPAS is a risk score U.S. courts have used to predict whether a defendant will reoffend, and it is known to flag Black defendants as high-risk at higher rates than white defendants with similar records. I rebuilt the risk classification with Random Forest and logistic regression, segmented defendant profiles with K-Means, and then tested what actually reduces the disparity: reweighing the training data, setting decision thresholds by group, and dropping race along with the variables that stand in for it.

scikit-learn · fairness methods
Research · Precision Interventions Lab · 2024–25Assessments for "The Science of Why"Designing the graded assessments for a 500-student causal inference course.

Dr. Falco Bargagli-Stoffi was teaching "The Science of Why," a causal inference course, for the first time, to more than 500 students, and needed assessments that would show where students were struggling. I designed them: chose the readings, wrote questions built to provoke debate rather than recall, and iterated the format with him until a read-a-document-then-answer style worked. The constraint that shaped it: students had AI tools, so the questions had to stay hard anyway. On the research side I cleaned and evaluated prostate cancer clinical trial data used to test one of the lab's methods.
